Preparedness app in development, own project

Nabolys

Nabolys helps a family and their neighbours get ready for the first three days of a crisis. It is my own project, and the app is on its way to Android and iPhone.

C#/.NET 10 · Azure SQL · Entra ID · Bicep · GitHub Actions · React Native · Kotlin · Swift

nabolys.azurewebsites.net ↗

Stock and three-day list

Type or scan your items with their best-before dates, and see what the household is missing for three days according to the authorities' advice.

Family plan and “I am OK”

Agree on a meeting place and important numbers, and check in with one tap, so the family can see you are fine.

Write and talk without a network

When the network is down, messages and voice go straight from phone to phone over Bluetooth, as long as you are within range.

Only the family can read it

What you share with the family is locked before it leaves your phone. Only the family can read it, not even us.

What does your household need?

The Danish authorities recommend that you can manage on your own for three days in a crisis. It is guidance, not a duty. This is the same calculation as in the app, and each group says where the numbers come from.

Sources: Styrelsen for Samfundssikkerhed, Fødevarestyrelsen and Nationalbanken. The authorities mention several things with no amount, and there the app counts at least one. Nabolys is a private project, not an authority.

A rota from a photo

Take a picture of the rota where it hangs. Weft reads the shifts and asks when it is unsure.

Weft works out the travel time from door to door and tells you when the train fails.

Nothing you cannot undo

Ordering platform in daily use, team project

Kvixa

Self-service kiosks and online ordering for grill bars, cafés and takeaway places. I co-developed it together with study friends and helped launch it at Chicken N Chicken Waves in Greve, where the orders now come in through Kvixa every day.

Node.js · MongoDB · Kotlin + Compose · Flutter · Docker

kvixa.vestegnensit.dk ↗ chickenwaves.dk ↗

In the shop

Guests order and pay at the screen themselves, with add-ons and meals, Danish and English, and the 14 EU allergens on every dish.

Online

The restaurant's own website and ordering page. Guests pick up now or later, from the same menu as the kiosk.

Behind the counter

Every order rings on one counter screen until someone taps Received. The numbers move from preparing to ready on an ordinary TV.

For the restaurant

Menu, prices and opening hours are edited from a phone, and one tap marks a dish as sold out.
